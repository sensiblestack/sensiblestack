The short answer

HighLevel (you will also see it written as GoHighLevel) sells one platform at three prices. There is no feature ladder to climb: the CRM, pipelines, funnel and website builders, email and SMS marketing, calendars and booking, forms, surveys, courses, communities, reputation tools and workflow automation are switched on at every tier. What the extra money buys is room to resell: more client sub-accounts, the right to rebill usage to those clients, and, at the top, SaaS mode, which turns the platform into a product you can sell under your own brand.

There is no long term contract: HighLevel's pricing page states the service runs month to month and can be upgraded, downgraded or cancelled from the dashboard. The free trial is 14 days, it takes a card, and it bills automatically at the end unless you cancel. Decide by day 13, not day 14.

Starter, $97 a month

The plan a single business should buy. You get every feature, unlimited contacts and unlimited users, and up to three sub-accounts, which in plain terms means three separate business workspaces under one subscription: your business, a side project, and one spare. HighLevel's own FAQ names the sub-account cap as the main difference between Starter and Unlimited, and from our own account we can confirm the cap is the only ceiling we have met. Nothing in the day to day toolset is held back.

Unlimited, $297 a month

The agency step. Sub-accounts become unlimited, so a fourth client is no longer a plan change. Unlimited also adds rebilling: the phone and email usage your clients generate can be passed through to them at cost, and basic API access for connecting other tools. If you have no clients, this tier buys you nothing you will use, because the features your own business runs on are identical to Starter's.

Agency Pro, $497 a month

The reseller tier. SaaS mode lets you sell HighLevel itself as your own software: clients sign up on your site, their accounts are created automatically, and you set the price. Rebilling gains a markup, so usage becomes a margin rather than a pass through, and API access steps up to the advanced level. This is a business model purchase, not a features purchase, and it only makes sense once selling software is the plan.

The costs on top of the headline price

HighLevel's own pricing FAQ is plain about this: the subscription covers the platform features, and telecommunications and AI carry usage charges. Those charges are paid from a prepaid wallet inside the account, and the wallet tops itself up from your card on file when the balance falls below its threshold, a detail worth knowing before your first $20 recharge surprises you. The rates below are the US figures from HighLevel's help centre, checked November 2026.

Phone numbers, texts and calls

  • A local number costs $1.15 a month, a toll free number $2.15 a month. No number is included in the subscription, and nothing in the phone system works until you rent one
  • Text messages cost $0.00747 per segment, sent or received, in the US and Canada. A segment is 160 characters, so a long text costs two or three segments, and the recipient's carrier adds its own fee on top, a few tenths of a cent per segment depending on the carrier
  • Calls cost $0.0166 a minute outbound and $0.01165 a minute inbound, billed in whole minutes rounded up. Calls forwarded to an ordinary phone number cost $0.02 a minute, because both legs are billed

Email

  • Sending through the built in LC-Email service costs $0.675 per 1,000 emails, on every plan. Incoming mail is charged too: every address in the To, CC and BCC fields counts
  • Optional email verification, which checks addresses before you send, costs $2.50 per 1,000 checks
  • From our own account: a brand new account sending on HighLevel's shared domain showed a cap of 500 emails a day. HighLevel's help centre explains why: sending limits ramp up over the first week, from 250 on day one, reaching 25,000 a day on a shared domain from day eight. A dedicated sending domain of your own lifts the ceiling much higher and gives you a reputation of your own, which is why our HighLevel setup guide recommends adding one in your first month

Texting registration: A2P 10DLC

In the US, business texts from a local number have to be registered under A2P 10DLC, and carriers block unregistered traffic. HighLevel's phone pricing guide publishes the registration charges: a one time fee of $22.50 for sole proprietor and low volume campaigns, or $64 for high volume, plus a monthly campaign fee of up to $2.10 for a sole proprietor, between $1.50 and $10.50 for low volume, and $10.50 for high volume. Both charges start when you submit, whatever the review decides, although campaign resubmissions have been free since February 2026.

Two first-hand points from our own registration screen. First, no fee and no approval time is shown anywhere inside the account until your business details are entered, so the figures above come from the published guide, not from anything our account displayed. Second, the registration is gated on paperwork: it asks for your legal business name exactly as it appears on your tax documents, your EIN, and an upload of the IRS confirmation letter, the CP-575. The screen also warns that an EIN issued in the last 15 days may be rejected. Have the letter in hand before you start, and expect the approval to run on the carriers' clock, in days rather than minutes.

AI features

The AI assistants that help you build, Ask AI, the studio and content tools, are included for agency users at no extra cost, subject to fair use. The AI agents that deal with customers are paid. Conversation AI, Voice AI and Reviews AI run pay per use, or under an AI Employee subscription priced per sub-account: Growth at $50 a month, which includes allowances such as 1,000 Conversation AI responses and 100 Voice AI minutes a month, or Unlimited at $97 a month for very high included usage. A cheaper $20 AI Employee Starter exists, but only for agencies on the $497 plan. Premium AI actions inside workflows, things like decision making and intent detection, cost $0.01 per execution, with Workflow Pro bundles from $10 a month for heavier users. A single business that is not putting an AI agent in front of its customers can ignore this whole section, and its bill will show it.

Which tier suits which business

A single business, or up to three workspaces: Starter. This is most of the businesses in our audience: a trades firm, a clinic, a coach, a local services company that wants its leads, bookings and follow up in one place. Every feature is present, and the $200 a month saved against Unlimited is real money at this size. It is also the tier we run ourselves.

An agency with four or more clients: Unlimited. The trigger is arithmetic, not ambition. The day a fourth sub-account is needed, Starter stops being available to you, and rebilling at cost keeps client usage off your own bill.

An agency selling software as its own product: Agency Pro. SaaS mode, automatic account creation and rebilling with a markup are the toolkit for selling HighLevel under your own brand. Nobody should buy this tier for a better CRM, because the CRM is the same one Starter has.

Our recommendation

Start on Starter, bill it annually once the trial convinces you, and treat the wallet as part of the price: roughly $120 of first year usage and registration at small business volumes, on top of the $970. Rent the phone number and start the A2P registration in your first week, because texting is the feature most businesses buy HighLevel for and it is the one gated behind paperwork and carrier timescales. Step up to Unlimited only when a fourth workspace forces the question, and leave Agency Pro until selling the platform itself is a real plan rather than an interesting idea.

If the breadth is more than you need, that is a legitimate answer too: a business that only wants email marketing, or only wants a CRM, will find calmer and cheaper tools in our reviews index.
