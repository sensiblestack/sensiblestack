Project Management Tools, Reviewed First Hand

Twelve project and task tools, tested against the same work: waterfall project management, budget control, task planning and Gantt charts, mostly solo, by a reviewer who has paid for, trialled or run all of them and kept exactly one. Every review is labelled Used it, Trialled it or Research only, scored on our ten measures, and rated by business type.

The reviews

  • Notion: the one tool that tries to be every tool, and the one our reviewer kept. Used it.
  • ClickUp: Notion's customisation without the homework, metered free tier. Used it.
  • monday.com: the one we wanted to pick, priced in threes. Used it.
  • Asana: solid, structured, priced for the team upstairs. Used it.
  • Motion: the calendar that rearranges your life, politely. Used it.
  • Wrike: deep scheduling, heavy steering. Used it.
  • Airtable: a database wearing a project tool's coat. Used it.
  • AITable.ai: the AI builds the spreadsheet. Mostly. Used it.
  • ProjectManager: Microsoft Project's manners, without the desktop. Trialled it.
  • Teamwork: built for client work, priced in two halves. Research only.
  • Coda, now Superhuman Docs: the thinking person's doc. Trialled it.
  • Todoist: a superb list, wandering around a project series. Used it.

Reading order if you are choosing: start with Notion and ClickUp if you work solo, monday.com and Asana if you lead a team, ProjectManager and Wrike if your schedules have baselines and teeth, and Motion if your problem is your calendar rather than your plan.
