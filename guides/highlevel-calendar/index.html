HighLevel Calendar Setup: The Settings That Decide Whether Bookings Work

The short answer: a HighLevel calendar takes bookings out of the box, but four settings decide whether it works in practice: the conflict calendars that prevent double bookings, the confirmation message your customer sees, the appointment notification toggle most people never check, and a workflow to guarantee the confirmation email. All four are in this guide, in the order to set them.

Try it: Visit HighLevel (affiliate link: we may be paid if you sign up, at no extra cost to you).

Before you share the link: rename it

Calendars created from a template keep the template's fingerprints. In our account the booking link still carried the template's name in its web address long after the calendar itself was renamed, and a link that names somebody else's template business looks unfinished to a customer. Open the calendar's booking link settings, set the address to your own business name, and only then put the link anywhere public. While you are there, set your availability hours, appointment duration and buffer time: the widget offers the soonest slot your rules allow, and in our test that was the next working morning, exactly as configured.

Step 1: set your conflict calendars

Double bookings in HighLevel rarely come from the calendar itself. They come from the calendar not knowing about your other commitments. Each calendar has a conflict setting: the list of other calendars it must check before offering a slot. Connect your personal calendar, mark which of its events count as Busy rather than Free, and add every other HighLevel calendar you take bookings on to the conflict list. An event marked Free is ignored when slots are calculated, which is correct for a tentative hold and a nasty surprise for a real appointment. Set this before your first booking, not after your first clash.

Step 2: read the confirmation page like a customer

Book a test appointment on your own calendar and read the confirmation page you land on. Ours printed a phone line pulled automatically from the Business Profile through a placeholder in the calendar's thank you message. The number it printed was the one held on the profile, which is not automatically the number you want this business's customers calling. Check three things: the number shown is this business's number, the wording matches what actually happens next, and the message does not promise a confirmation email unless you have set one up, which is the next step. If the profile phone is blank, the placeholder prints nothing, and the sentence around it reads oddly, so write the message to survive an empty field.

Step 3: the appointment notification is a toggle, and it may be off

Here is the finding from our own test booking. The calendar's own "Appointment booked" email notification was disabled in our account, and no confirmation email from the calendar ever arrived. The booking was real, it sat on the calendar with status Confirmed, and the customer side of it was silent. Find the notification setting on your calendar, switch the appointment email on, and prove it with a second test booking to an inbox you control. If you would rather control the wording yourself, leave it off deliberately and cover the confirmation with a workflow instead, which is step 4. What you cannot do is assume it is on.

Step 4: guarantee the confirmation with a workflow

A workflow triggered by a new appointment is the reliable route: it sends your wording, from your address, every time, and it leaves an enrolment record you can check when a customer says nothing arrived. We built ours in about four minutes and it fired on the test booking within seconds. The settings that decide whether a workflow fires at all, including one default that surprises everyone, are in our HighLevel workflow guide.

Step 5: when the calendar looks empty, check the view filter

A trap from our own testing, worth knowing before it costs you an hour. The calendar week view can show nothing at all while a confirmed booking exists, because the calendar itself is unticked in the view filter at the side of the page. Before concluding a booking has vanished, tick the calendar back on in the filter. The booking was there the whole time.

What a booking does to your contacts

One reassuring behaviour: a booking does not blindly create a new contact. In our tests, both a booking and a form submission matched the existing contact by email address and updated it, so the contact count did not grow. The caution that comes with it: the most recent submission wins. A later form filled in with a different name overwrote the name the booking had set, and the phone number from the form was added to the same record. Convenient for keeping one record per person, worth knowing before you wonder why a contact's name changed.

The five minute checklist

  • Booking link renamed to your business, hours, duration and buffers set
  • Conflict calendars connected, real commitments marked Busy
  • Confirmation page read as a customer, phone number and wording checked
  • Appointment notification proved on, or a workflow confirmation in its place
  • One full test booking made, and the calendar view filter left ticked on
