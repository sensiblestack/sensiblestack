Affiliate Disclosure

Last updated November 2026. This page is written to satisfy the spirit of the FTC and ASA disclosure rules, not just the letter: if money changes hands anywhere near a recommendation, you should be able to see exactly where.

The short version

  • Some links on this site are affiliate links. If you sign up through one, the vendor pays us, at no extra cost to you. You pay the vendor's standard price.
  • Every review and comparison carries a disclosure at the top, and comparison pages name which links earn and which are plain.
  • Scores follow one published method and cannot be bought, raised or softened by any payment, ever.
  • Several of our strongest recommendations earn us nothing at all, and we say so on the page.
  • We run no advertising, sell no placements, and publish no sponsored reviews.

How the money actually moves

Affiliate links on this site are marked rel="sponsored" and pass through our own /go/ addresses, so we can count clicks and fix a link in one place if a vendor changes it. When you click one, the vendor's site sets a cookie that attributes your visit to us. If you then sign up, and in most programmes only when you become a paying customer, the vendor pays us a fee. Depending on the programme that fee is a share of your subscription for a set period, or a one time payment on your first purchase. Attribution windows vary by programme, from 60 days to 12 months.

What we see at our end is deliberately thin: that an anonymous referral signed up, for which product, and on what date. We never see your name, your email address, your payment details or anything you do inside the tool. The vendor knows who its customer is; we know that a reader clicked and converted, and nothing more.

Occasionally an affiliate link works in your favour rather than just ours. A Make referral, for example, starts with a free month of its Core plan. Where a link carries a reader benefit like that, the review says so.

What money does, and what it never does

Honesty needs precision here, because "money never affects anything" is a claim nobody should believe from any review site. This is the exact position.

What it can affect: presentation, in one narrow case. Our standing rule is that where two tools finish genuinely level in a verdict, the one with an affiliate programme may get the more prominent button. That is the whole extent of it, and it is why we would rather state it than pretend otherwise.

What it never affects: scores, rankings, verdicts, or a word of criticism. Every tool is scored on the same ten measures and rated by business type under the method on our How We Rate page, and the scoring happens before links are placed. No vendor can pay for inclusion, for a better score, for a softer write up, or to have a fault removed. Faults stay in, including in reviews of programmes that pay us.

The proof is on the site. Klaviyo has no affiliate programme at all and wins its comparisons outright. HubSpot declined our application and we still recommend its free CRM where it fits, on plain links. If a paying programme's tool is wrong for your business type, its review says so, in those words.

How the recommendations are built

Sensible Stack is a one person business, and the testing and the writing are done by the same person, which removes the usual separation problem rather than creating one: there is no sales team to lean on the reviewer, because there is no sales team. Reviews are labelled by how the knowledge was earned: Used it for tools run in a real business, Trialled it for tools put through a structured trial, and Research only where the verdict rests on published specifications and reviewer consensus, stated as such. A research only tool can be recommended, but it can never be described as tested. Our aim is to test everything on the site over time, and research only labels are upgraded as each tool is trialled.

Sponsored content: we publish none. If that ever changes, the piece will be labelled Sponsored at the top, before the first paragraph, and it will not carry a score.
