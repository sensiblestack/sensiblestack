Mid Sized Businesses

Software that survives departments, seats and approvals.

At twenty one to a hundred people, software choices stop being personal. There are departments with different needs, a finance person asking about seat counts and renewal terms, and reporting that has to roll up from team level to something an executive will read. First impressions matter less here. Portfolios, permissions, integrations and what it costs to leave matter more.

Top rated for mid sized businesses

From our twelve project and task tool reviews, scored for this size of business:

  • Asana: 9. Portfolios, goals and workload built for exactly this shape of company.
  • Wrike: 9. The deepest scheduling and resource planning in the series, priced in seat bundles.
  • monday.com: 8. The adoption friendly option, if the three seat minimums stop mattering at your scale.
  • ProjectManager: 8. For the programmes with baselines, dependencies and Microsoft Project files moving in and out.
  • Teamwork: 8. If the hundred people mostly deliver client work.
  • ClickUp and Coda (Superhuman Docs): 7 each. Capable, with more admin overhead in the setup.

The stack question at this size

It stops being "which tools" and becomes "how few". One project platform, not one per department. A CRM with governance rather than a spreadsheet with ambitions: HubSpot vs the CRM field. Hosting with a support record you can defend in a meeting: Kinsta vs WP Engine. Email with list hygiene enforced, because profile based pricing at this size is a real line item: Kit vs Klaviyo.

What to avoid

Shadow tools bought on a card by one department. Renewal terms nobody read. And AI features metered by credits with no owner: somebody in the business should know what the meter costs when it runs dry.
