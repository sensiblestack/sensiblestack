Growing Businesses

Systems that stop breaking as you hire.

Between six and twenty people, memory stops working as a filing system. Handovers replace hallway conversations, the spreadsheet that ran the business becomes the bottleneck, and somebody senior starts asking for a dashboard instead of a status meeting. The software question changes from "is it easy" to "does it still work when three teams lean on it at once".

Top rated for growing businesses

From our twelve project and task tool reviews, scored for this size of business:

  • monday.com: 9. Non technical hires adopt it without a fight, and it holds up as teams multiply.
  • Asana: 8. Portfolios and workload arrive exactly when this size starts needing them.
  • ClickUp: 8. Depth across tasks, docs and dashboards in one bill.
  • Coda (Superhuman Docs): 8. Makers build the trackers, everyone else just uses them.
  • ProjectManager: 8. When schedules gain baselines and clients who ask for the file.
  • Teamwork: 8. If the growth is client work, its shape matches your economics.

The starter stack at this size

A CRM you will not outgrow by Christmas: HubSpot vs HighLevel is the honest version of that decision. One project platform with real dashboards, from the list above. An email platform chosen for the list you will have next year, not the one you have now: Kit vs Klaviyo. Hosting with support that answers at your new traffic levels: Kinsta vs WP Engine.

What to avoid

Free tiers held together with exports and good intentions. Tools whose price triples at the next tier with no warning in the small print. And buying AI seats for everyone before anyone has a use for them: check the credit meter, because there usually is one.
