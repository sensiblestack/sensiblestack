Your Make credits are disappearing, and nothing is broken

Your automations have done almost no work, but the credit balance keeps falling. The likeliest cause is not a fault and not a leak. It is your scenarios politely asking "anything new?" several thousand times a month, and being charged for every ask.

Fix series, post one. Checked against Make's own help centre.

The symptom

You log into Make and the credit meter has moved, a lot, on days when the automation plainly did nothing. No new rows processed, no messages sent, no records created. On the free plan, which comes with 1,000 credits a month, a single scenario can quietly eat the whole month. On a paid plan the same thing shows up as a balance that never lasts as long as your maths said it should.

The cause: every check costs a credit

Most scenarios start with a trigger module whose name begins with "Watch": Watch New Rows, Watch Records, Watch Emails. Those are what Make calls polling triggers. On a schedule, the scenario wakes up and asks the other app whether anything new has arrived.

Here is the part that catches people out, in Make's own words from its help centre: a trigger module runs once to check for or retrieve data, regardless of how many bundles come back. Found nothing? That check still costs one credit. Make's own worked example is a Google Sheets Watch New Rows module: one operation per check, every check, empty or not.

Now do the arithmetic on the default schedule, which checks every 15 minutes:

  • Every 15 minutes: 96 checks a day, about 2,880 credits a month, per scenario.
  • Every hour: about 720 credits a month.
  • Once a day: about 30 credits a month.
  • And if you set a scenario to check every minute, you are paying for roughly 43,200 checks a month before it has done a single useful thing.

Two or three polling scenarios on default settings can burn more credits standing idle than a busy automation spends working. Nothing is malfunctioning. This is the pricing model working exactly as designed, which is why it is so annoying.

The fix, in the order to try it

1. Find the drain before you fix anything. In Make, open your organisation's usage view and the Scenarios list, and see which scenarios are consuming the credits. Then look at each hungry scenario's first module. If its name starts with "Watch" and it carries no INSTANT tag or lightning icon, it is a polling trigger, and it is charging you for every look.

2. Swap polling for instant wherever the app offers it. Instant triggers work the other way round: the app notifies Make when something actually happens, through a webhook, so the scenario only runs when there is work to do. An instant trigger costs nothing while nothing is happening. Look for the same app's trigger marked INSTANT. Where an app has no instant trigger, a webhook or mailhook module can do the same job for form and email sources.

3. If polling has to stay, slow it down to match the job. In the scenario's Schedule settings, pick the slowest interval the work tolerates. Does that spreadsheet really need checking every 15 minutes, or would hourly, or twice a day, change nothing for the business? The table above is the whole decision: the interval is the bill.

4. If several scenarios watch the same source, merge them. One polling trigger, then a Router splitting the flow into the different jobs. You pay for one set of checks instead of three or four.

5. Trim what happens after the trigger. Put filters immediately after the trigger module so unwanted items stop early: filters, routers and error handlers cost no credits themselves. And where a scenario processes many items one at a time before a single action, an aggregator can combine them into one bundle first. The polling fix is the big one, but long scenarios multiply every item they touch, and this is where that bill is cut.

The honest limits

Some apps genuinely offer no instant trigger, and for those, polling at a sensible interval is not a mistake, it is the only option. Polling is also the right choice when the source cannot push notifications at all. The mistake is never polling itself. The mistake is a 15 minute default, chosen by nobody, running against a job that changes twice a day.
