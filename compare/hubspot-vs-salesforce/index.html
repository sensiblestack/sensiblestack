HubSpot vs Salesforce: The CRM Decision for Businesses of 1 to 100

HubSpotvsSalesforce

Try it: Visit HubSpot.

We Used It HQ: Streamlining business, whatever your size

The short answer

If you are a business of 1 to 100 people choosing a first real CRM, HubSpot, and it is not particularly close. You can start free, set it up yourself, and have marketing, sales and service in one place by the end of the week.

If your sales process is genuinely complicated, with custom objects, multiple pipelines feeding each other, or an industry tool that only exists on Salesforce's AppExchange, Salesforce earns its keep even at small size.

If you are choosing Salesforce mainly because it is the famous name, or because you expect to be a 500 person company one day, stop. For most businesses in our size range its scale stops being an asset and starts being a bill: per user pricing on tiers built for machinery a 30 person firm never switches on, plus admin time to look after it.

What each one is

HubSpot is a CRM platform organized into hubs: Marketing, Sales, Service and more, all sitting on one contact database. The free CRM is the front door, and it is a real product, not a demo. Paid tiers add automation, reporting and volume, priced per seat, with marketing also priced by how many marketing contacts you hold.

Salesforce is the original cloud CRM, and still the deepest. Sales Cloud is the core product most people mean; marketing, service and commerce are separate clouds sold on top. Almost anything a sales organization can imagine doing, Salesforce can be made to do, which is both the selling point and the catch: making it do those things is a project, often a paid one.

Pricing at a realistic small business size

US dollars, from the vendors' published pricing, checked November 2026. Take a five person sales team as the test case:

  • HubSpot Sales Hub Starter: five seats at the $20 list price is $100 a month. New customers currently see a lower introductory offer, $7 a seat billed annually, which is $35 a month; budget on the list price, because offers end.
  • HubSpot Sales Hub Professional: five seats at $90 a month billed annually is $450 a month, plus a mandatory one off onboarding fee of $1,500. Year one is $6,900 before tax.
  • Salesforce Starter Suite: five users at $25 is $125 a month. Pro Suite, the tier with forecasting and quoting, is five at $100: $500 a month, billed annually. There is no mandatory onboarding fee, but small firms commonly pay a consultant to set Salesforce up, and that cost is real even though Salesforce does not publish it.
  • Marketing on top: HubSpot Marketing Hub Starter includes 1,000 marketing contacts; at roughly 2,000 contacts you add a block of 1,000 at $50 a month, so one seat and 2,000 contacts runs about $70 a month at list. Marketing Hub Professional is a different world: $800 a month billed annually, plus $3,000 onboarding. On Salesforce, marketing automation is a separate product priced on top of Sales Cloud, so the comparison is never just the Sales Cloud license.

The shape is the story: HubSpot starts free and stays cheap until the Professional cliff, which is steep and arrives suddenly. Salesforce starts at $25 a user and climbs a steadier ladder, $25, $100, then $175 a user a month for Enterprise, but the license is rarely the whole bill.

Where HubSpot wins

  • The free tier: a working CRM, forms and landing pages for nothing, which is how this business started with it
  • One database: marketing, sales and service looking at the same contact record, no syncing between clouds
  • Setup you can do yourself: no implementation partner, no six week project
  • An interface a non specialist can learn without training days

Where Salesforce wins

  • Customization depth: custom objects, complex approval chains, territory structures, almost no ceiling
  • AppExchange: if your industry has a specialist tool, it probably connects to Salesforce first
  • Reporting at complexity: forecasting across teams, products and regions in ways HubSpot only reaches at its top tiers
  • Staying power: a business that truly will grow past a few hundred staff will not outgrow it

Where each one frustrates

HubSpot: the cliff. Starter at $20 a seat is gentle; Professional, where sequences, proper automation and forecasting live, is $90 a seat for Sales Hub and $800 a month for Marketing Hub, with mandatory onboarding fees on top, and Professional is priced for annual billing, with a pricier monthly option. Marketing contacts pricing also means a growing list quietly raises the bill. Plenty of small businesses feel the jump is designed to hurt, and on the numbers, it is hard to argue.

Salesforce: the weight. For a business of 1 to 100 people, most of what Enterprise and Unlimited sell, territory management, advanced workflow across business units, deep developer customization, is machinery you will never switch on, billed per user every month. Somebody has to administer it, and in a small firm that somebody is usually the owner, in the evenings. Where Salesforce's scale stops making sense for this audience is roughly here: if you cannot name the specific complex process that needs it, you are paying for headroom, not capability.

If you leave

Records move either way by CSV: contacts, companies and deals import cleanly into both tools. Everything built on top of the records is the cost. HubSpot workflows, sequences and custom reports rebuild in Salesforce, and Salesforce flows, consultant built customizations and reports rebuild in HubSpot. Email engagement history does not travel in either direction. HubSpot adds two exit frictions Salesforce does not: the annual term runs to renewal before a downgrade bites, and the marketing contacts tier ratchets up mid term when your list grows.

Verdict

Choose HubSpot if you are under 100 people, want one system for contacts, marketing and sales, need to be running this week rather than this quarter, and would rather spend on seats than on setup. Start on the free tools, which are better than most paid starter CRMs, and watch the Professional cliff with your eyes open.

Choose Salesforce if your sales process is the complicated part of your business: multiple teams or territories, custom objects a normal CRM cannot model, an AppExchange tool your industry runs on, or a credible plan to grow well past 100 staff where migrating later would cost more than starting there. If none of those sentences describes you, Salesforce is a famous name doing an ordinary job at an extraordinary price.
