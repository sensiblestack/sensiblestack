HubSpot vs Zoho: Polish and Ecosystem Against the Whole Suite for Less

HubSpotvsZoho

Try it: Visit HubSpot.

We Used It HQ: Streamlining business, whatever your size

The short answer

If you want the CRM your team will actually enjoy opening every morning, and your list is small enough to live inside the free tools or the Starter tier, HubSpot. It is the most polished small business CRM we have used, and the free version is genuinely useful rather than a teaser.

If you want one subscription that covers CRM, email campaigns, accounting, support tickets, projects and more, for less than HubSpot charges for marketing alone once your list grows, Zoho, and it is not close on price.

If you are a one person business choosing a first CRM today, start on HubSpot free. Move to Zoho when the HubSpot bill, not the features, becomes the problem. The pricing section below shows exactly where that line sits.

What each one is

HubSpot is a customer platform built around a free CRM, with paid hubs layered on top for marketing, sales and service. The free CRM, forms and landing page builder are the front door, and they are good enough that plenty of small businesses never pay a penny. The catch is in how the paid marketing tier counts: you pay per seat, and you pay again for the contacts you are allowed to email.

Zoho is a software company that sells almost everything a small business runs on, app by app or all at once. Zoho CRM is the flagship, and Zoho One wraps more than 50 of its apps, CRM, campaigns, accounting, helpdesk, projects, HR and more, into one per person price. The catch is polish: the apps work, and they integrate with each other, but the interfaces are denser and the learning curve is real.

Pricing at a real small business size

Checked against vendor pricing pages and HubSpot's published product catalogue, November 2026. Take one realistic business: five people, everyone in the CRM, two of them sending campaigns, and about 2,000 contacts it actually emails. That last number matters, because it is where HubSpot's pricing model starts to bite.

HubSpot. The CRM itself is free for up to two users. Marketing Hub Starter lists at $20 per seat per month (a lower promotional price for new customers comes and goes, so plan on the list price). Starter includes 1,000 marketing contacts. At 2,000 contacts you buy one extra block of 1,000, at $50 a month. So two marketing seats and 2,000 contacts cost $40 plus $50: $90 a month. Contacts you hold but do not email are free to store, which softens the bill if much of your list is dormant. The problem is the next step up. The automation most growing businesses eventually want lives in Marketing Hub Professional: $800 a month billed annually, or $890 monthly, with three seats and 2,000 contacts included, plus a mandatory one off onboarding fee of $3,000. There is no tier in between. Your bill goes from $90 a month to $800 a month because you outgrew one feature list.

Zoho. Zoho CRM is free for up to three users. Paid editions are per user per month, billed annually: Standard $14, Professional $23, Enterprise $40, Ultimate $52. All five people on Professional, the edition most small teams land on, cost $115 a month, and there is no contact block to buy at any list size. Or skip the app by app math entirely: Zoho One, the whole suite of more than 50 apps, is $37 per employee per month billed annually on the All Employee plan, which requires licensing everyone on the payroll, or $90 per user per month on the Flexible User plan if only some people need it. Five people on All Employee cost $185 a month for CRM, campaigns, accounting, helpdesk, projects and the rest, in one bill.

The shape is the story: HubSpot is cheaper while you are small and your ambitions fit in Starter, then jumps a cliff. Zoho costs a little more than HubSpot's entry point and then simply grows one user at a time.

Where HubSpot wins

  • The free tools: a free CRM, form builder and landing page builder good enough to launch a real business on, which is exactly what we did. A form that has to take a payment is a different job: see the Jotform review
  • Ease of use: the interface sets the standard for the category, and a team that finds its CRM pleasant keeps its data clean
  • The ecosystem: a marketplace of integrations so wide that whatever else you adopt, it will almost certainly connect
  • Guidance built in: reporting, templates and prompts that quietly teach a first time operator what good looks like

Where Zoho wins

  • Value, by a distance: a full CRM for five people costs less than HubSpot charges two people to email 2,000 contacts
  • The suite: Zoho One replaces separate bills for accounting, helpdesk, projects, HR and campaigns with one per person price
  • No contact tax: your list can grow to any size without the CRM bill noticing
  • Depth per dollar: automation, custom modules and inventory reach down into editions that cost less than a round of coffees per user

Where each one frustrates

HubSpot: the pricing architecture. Per seat and per contact block and per hub is three meters running at once, the Starter to Professional step is roughly nine times the money plus a $3,000 onboarding fee, and downgrading a contact tier waits until renewal. None of this is hidden, exactly, but you feel each piece of it only after you are settled in.

Zoho: the finish. Menus are dense, settings live in surprising places, and the help documentation assumes more patience than most solo operators have on a Tuesday. The mobile apps and the newer interfaces are better than the reputation, but nobody has ever opened Zoho and called it beautiful. Budget setup time, or budget for a freelancer to set it up, which is still cheaper than the HubSpot cliff.

If you leave

Zoho's low price is not, by itself, lock in protection: records export from both tools, and automations, campaign history and reports rebuild in either direction. HubSpot adds frictions Zoho does not. The annual term runs to renewal before a downgrade takes effect, and the marketing contacts tier steps up mid term when your list grows past it, whether or not your revenue did.

Verdict

Choose HubSpot if your list is small and valuable, your team needs a CRM it will genuinely enjoy using, and you can see your needs staying inside the free tools or Starter for the foreseeable future. Start free, it costs nothing to find out, and the landing page builder alone earned its keep for us.

Choose Zoho if the math above made you wince, if your list is big or growing fast, or if you are paying for three or more separate tools that Zoho One would swallow into one bill. You trade polish for an enormous amount of software per dollar, and for most businesses of 1 to 100 people that trade is the sensible one.
