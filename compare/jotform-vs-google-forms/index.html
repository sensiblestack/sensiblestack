Jotform vs Google Forms: The Free Default Against the Builder That Does More

The short answer

If the form is a survey, a quiz, an RSVP list or a quick poll, and the answers belong in a spreadsheet, Google Forms. It costs nothing, it is already sitting inside the Google account you use every day, and there is nothing new to learn.

If the form has to take money, collect files from people who do not have Google accounts, work without an internet connection, or change its questions based on earlier answers, Jotform. That list is exactly what the subscription buys.

If you are a one person business choosing today, start on Google Forms for casual and internal forms, and open a free Jotform account the day a form needs a payment field. The free Jotform tier is the full builder with tight limits, so the trial tells you the truth about the paid product.

What each one is

Google Forms is Google's form tool, part of the same account as Gmail, Drive and Sheets, free for anyone with a Google account and included in Google Workspace. Its strength is the absence of friction: build a form in minutes, share a link, and watch the answers arrive in a Google Sheet. Its ceiling is just as clear. Google's own list of question types has no payment type in it, and the logic stops at sending people to different sections.

Jotform is a dedicated form builder that has spent two decades adding everything Google left out: payment fields, deep conditional logic, file uploads with real storage behind them, a mobile app that works offline, and compliance tiers for health data. The catch is metering. Almost everything is counted, forms, submissions, form views, payment submissions and storage all have their own ceilings, and every plan below Enterprise covers exactly one user.

Pricing at a real small business size

Checked against Jotform's pricing page and Google's published help pages, November 2026. Take one realistic business: a solo operator with a contact form, a booking form and an order form that takes card payments, collecting a few hundred submissions a month.

Google Forms. The price is $0, and for the contact form and the booking form it is genuinely all you need. The order form is where it stops: there is no way to take the payment inside the form, at any price, because Google does not sell a version with one. The meters that do exist are generous. Google publishes a limit of 500,000 responses per form and 1 GB of form data, and uploads draw on your Google account storage, 15 GB free, shared with Gmail and Photos. Almost no small business will ever touch those ceilings.

Jotform. The free Starter tier covers 5 forms and 100 submissions a month, which the business above outgrows quickly. Bronze, at $34 a month billed annually, raises that to 25 forms, 1,000 submissions a month, 1 GB of space and 100 payment submissions a month, and removes the Jotform branding. Silver, at $39 a month billed annually, gives 50 forms, 2,500 submissions, 10 GB and 250 payment submissions. Gold, at $99 a month billed annually, gives 100 forms, 10,000 submissions, 100 GB, 1,000 payment submissions and the HIPAA enabled features. Monthly billing exists on every tier and the annual toggle saves up to 20 percent, so the annual figures are the ones to budget on. Every plan from Starter to Gold covers one user; a team that needs several logins is looking at Enterprise, at a custom price. Until 30 November 2026 it also carries Jotform's Black Friday offer, 60% off annual Bronze, Silver and Gold plans, per Jotform's partner email of 19 November 2026.

The shape is the story: Google Forms is free and stays free, right up to the moment you need it to do something it simply does not do. Jotform charges from the first serious form, and the bill buys capability, not headroom alone.

Where Jotform wins

  • Payments inside the form: orders, donations and bookings complete in one step, through the gateway you already use, with no extra Jotform transaction fee
  • Logic depth: calculated fields, fields that appear only when relevant, and pages that skip themselves keep long forms short for the person filling them in
  • Offline collection: the mobile app takes responses at a site visit, a market stall or a trade show with no signal, and syncs later
  • Uploads without a wall: respondents can send you files without owning or signing into a Google account
  • A compliance tier exists: health and other sensitive forms have somewhere to go, on Gold, without changing tools

Where Google Forms wins

  • Price, outright: no subscription, no per submission meter running, and published limits most small businesses will never reach
  • Zero learning curve: if you can write an email you can build the form, and your respondents already know what a Google form is
  • Sheets is the back end: responses land in a spreadsheet you already know how to filter, chart and share, with a CSV download when you need one
  • Respondent friendly touches: a signed in respondent who gets interrupted finds their draft saved for 30 days
  • No account sprawl: one less login, one less bill and one less renewal date for a tool you might use twice a month

Where each one frustrates

Jotform: the meters, plural. Forms, monthly submissions, form views, payment submissions and storage are all capped separately, so a busy month can trip a limit you were not watching. It is also one user per plan until Enterprise, which surprises teams. And a quieter trap we have written up in our fix journal: Jotform notification emails can stop arriving entirely when an address lands on the bounce list, and the test button will not tell you. The fix takes two minutes, once you know it exists.

Google Forms: the absences. No payments, no calculations, no field level logic, and an upload question that turns away anyone without a Google account. The customisation ceiling is low, a colour, a header image and a font, so a customer facing form never quite looks like yours. None of this is a secret, but each absence tends to announce itself mid project rather than at the start.

If you leave

Google Forms data is easy to take with you: responses download as a CSV and live in Sheets in the first place. What does not travel is the form's behaviour, because there is barely any behaviour to travel. Jotform submissions export too, but a Jotform form rebuilt elsewhere means rebuilding its conditions, calculations and payment setup by hand. The deeper your logic, the more the form itself becomes the lock in, on either side of this comparison.

Verdict

Choose Google Forms if your forms ask questions and collect answers, your team lives in Google Workspace or free Google accounts, and no form needs to take a payment. It is the rare tool where the free option is not a teaser. For surveys, feedback, quizzes and internal requests, paying for a form builder buys you nothing you will use.

Choose Jotform if a form is part of how the business runs: an order form, a booking form with a deposit, an application that collects documents, a health intake form, a site survey completed offline. Bronze at $34 a month billed annually is the sensible entry point for most businesses of 1 to 100 people, and the free tier is generous enough to prove the fit before you pay it.
