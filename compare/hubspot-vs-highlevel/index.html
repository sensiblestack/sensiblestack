HubSpot vs HighLevel: Which CRM Is Right for a One Person Business?

Sensible Stack: Streamlining business, whatever your size

Disclosure: This page contains affiliate links. If you buy through them, we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. This review is a research based comparison of published features, pricing and terms. It is not a claim that we run our business on either platform.

The short answer

Choose HubSpot if you are a growing team that wants a polished CRM, strong reporting and a large app marketplace, and you accept that costs rise as you add seats and hubs.

Choose HighLevel if you are a solopreneur or small service business who wants CRM, website and funnel tools, email and SMS, calendars and automations in one flat subscription, including white label options for agencies.

What each one is

HubSpot is a CRM platform built around separate hubs: Marketing, Sales, Service, CMS and Operations. The CRM itself has a well known free tier. Paid value comes from the hubs, priced per seat, so a team pays more as it grows.

HighLevel, also called GoHighLevel, is an all in one platform aimed at agencies and local businesses. One subscription bundles CRM, funnels and websites, email and SMS, calendars and booking, pipelines, reputation tools and workflow automation. Its agency plans let agencies resell the platform under their own brand.

Pricing shape, and why it matters more than the headline

HighLevel pricing is flat by plan: Starter $97, Unlimited $297 and Founder $497 per month (names checked on the live pricing page 10 November 2026; HighLevel's own FAQ also calls the top tier Pro, so its naming is inconsistent), with usage costs such as SMS and email on top. A small team can predict the bill.

HubSpot pricing is modular. Starting prices look low, but each hub and each seat adds up, and larger tiers require onboarding fees on some plans. A growing sales team can find the annual bill far higher than the entry price suggested. That is not a flaw in the product, it is a different model: you pay for depth as you scale.

Where HubSpot wins

  • Reporting and dashboards that managers actually use
  • A very large integration marketplace
  • A free CRM that is genuinely usable for a first pipeline
  • Training, certification and community depth

Where HighLevel wins

  • Everything in one login, no stitching five tools together
  • SMS, email, calendars and automations included in the core product, not bought hub by hub
  • Flat pricing that suits a local service business or a small agency
  • White label reselling on the agency SaaS plan, which HubSpot does not offer in the same way

Where each one frustrates people

HubSpot: seat based pricing, tier jumps, and paying for contacts and hubs you grow into faster than expected.

HighLevel: a steeper learning curve, a busier interface, and more setup responsibility on you. It rewards businesses that will spend a weekend configuring it properly.

Choose the other one if

Choose HubSpot over HighLevel if you have a sales team, need board level reporting, or live inside a large app ecosystem.

Choose HighLevel over HubSpot if you are a local business or agency, send a lot of SMS and email, book appointments for a living, and want one predictable subscription.

Ratings by business type

HubSpot: Solo 7, Micro team 8, Growing business 9, Mid sized 9, Agency 7, Online seller 7, Creator 6. The free CRM lifts the solo score, seat pricing as you grow is the tax on its depth.

HighLevel: Solo 8, Micro team 9, Growing business 8, Mid sized 6, Agency 10, Online seller 6, Creator 5. Flat pricing and the all in one bundle are built for small operators and agencies first.

Best suited to: HubSpot, professional services, B2B and SaaS businesses with a real sales pipeline. HighLevel, home services and trades, clinics, realtors, local services that live on bookings, and marketing agencies.

Verdict

For a one person service business or a small agency: HighLevel, on value and completeness.

For a growing team with dedicated sales and marketing staff: HubSpot, on depth and reporting.

Both offer trials or free entry points, so test with your real pipeline and your real contacts list before you commit a year of budget.
