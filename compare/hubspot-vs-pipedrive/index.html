HubSpot vs Pipedrive: The Platform or the Pipeline

The short answer

If your problem is a sales pipeline and nothing else, Pipedrive. It is a focused tool that does that one job simply, and a small team can be working in it the same afternoon.

If your problem is bigger than the pipeline, leads arriving from a website, marketing email, follow up, customer service, all of it needing one home, HubSpot. The CRM there is one part of a platform, and the free tier is genuinely usable.

If you are a one person business with a handful of deals a month, start on HubSpot's free CRM and pay nobody anything until the pipeline itself hurts. That is the order most small businesses should do this in.

What each one is

HubSpot is a customer platform. The free CRM holds your contacts, companies and deals, and around it sit hubs for marketing, sales, service and content, each free in a basic form and paid in tiers. You can run a landing page, a contact database, an email list and a deal pipeline in one login, which is exactly how we first used it: a free landing page that looked premium, feeding a free CRM, for a business that could justify paying for neither.

Pipedrive is a sales pipeline tool and makes no apology for it. Deals move along a visual board, activities and follow ups are prompted rather than left to memory, and email syncs in from Growth tier up. It does not want to run your marketing, your website or your helpdesk. It wants your next deal closed, and everything in the interface points at that.

Pricing at a realistic team size

Checked against vendor pricing pages and published comparisons, November 2026. Both tools price per seat. HubSpot's paid tiers are bought as the Starter Customer Platform or as individual hubs; Pipedrive renamed its plans and now sells Lite, Growth, Premium and Ultimate.

  • Solo or a team of two: HubSpot free, $0, covers 2 users and up to 1,000 contacts, with HubSpot branding on what you send. Pipedrive has no free plan: Lite is $14 per seat a month billed annually, but Lite has no email sync and no automations, so the honest starting point is Growth at $39 per seat a month billed annually ($49 monthly).
  • A team of three: HubSpot Starter lists at $20 per seat a month, so $60 a month at list price, with new customer offers well below that at the time of checking. Pipedrive Growth for three seats is $117 a month billed annually.
  • A team of five, selling seriously: Pipedrive Growth is $195 a month billed annually, Premium $295. HubSpot Starter is $100 a month at list for five seats, but the sales features most teams picture, sequences and proper forecasting, sit in Sales Hub Professional at $90 per seat a month billed annually, $450 for five, plus a one off onboarding fee of $1,500.

The shape is the story: HubSpot is free, then cheap, then expensive in one jump. Pipedrive costs money from day one and then climbs in even steps you can predict.

Where HubSpot wins

  • The free CRM: real contact, company and deal management for two users, free for as long as you need it
  • Breadth: landing pages, forms, email, ads and service tickets share one database, so nothing is copied between tools
  • The landing page builder: the free page we ran looked like a paid product, and setup took an afternoon
  • Growing room: when marketing and sales genuinely merge in a business, HubSpot is already where both live

Where Pipedrive wins

  • Focus: the pipeline is the product, and it shows in every screen. Nothing competes for your attention
  • Speed of adoption: a salesperson can learn it in a day, which is the real reason small teams keep it updated
  • Honest tiering: four plans, per seat, no contact blocks, no seat types, no onboarding fee
  • Activity prompts: follow ups are scheduled and chased by the tool, which is where small pipelines usually leak

Where each one frustrates

HubSpot: the pricing staircase. Starter is reasonable and Professional is a cliff, $90 a seat for Sales Hub with a mandatory onboarding fee, and the features are dealt across hubs, seats and contact blocks in a way that takes real effort to price before you commit. The free tools also carry HubSpot branding, and the platform is wide enough that a small business can spend more time arranging it than using it.

Pipedrive: the edges of the job. Email marketing is a paid add on rather than a citizen, lead capture leans on add ons below Premium, and the moment your problem includes a website, a newsletter or a support inbox, you are shopping for other tools anyway. It is also paid from the first seat, with only a 14 day trial to decide by.

If you leave

Pipedrive is the easier of the two to walk away from: pipeline data exports cleanly, and there is simply less platform to unwind. Leaving HubSpot means waiting out the annual term if you want to downgrade first, and rebuilding workflows, sequences, forms and reports wherever you land. The records export fine. The machine you built around them does not.

Verdict

Choose HubSpot if your leads arrive through marketing, a website, a list, a landing page, and the pipeline is one stop on a longer journey. Start free, stay free while two seats and 1,000 contacts cover you, and budget honestly for the Professional jump before you ever need it.

Choose Pipedrive if selling is a defined job in your business, deals are quoted, chased and closed by people, and what you need is a pipeline the team will actually maintain. Skip Lite, start on Growth, and accept that marketing tools live elsewhere.

One person, deals occasional, budget zero: neither paid plan. HubSpot free, and revisit the question when the pipeline, not the software, is the bottleneck.
