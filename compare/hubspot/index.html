HubSpot vs the CRM field

HubSpotvsSalesforce · Zoho · Pipedrive · Freshsales · Copper · Keap · monday CRM

Try it: Visit HubSpot (affiliate link: we may be paid if you sign up, at no extra cost to you).

HubSpot is the friendly face of CRM: a genuinely good free tier, landing pages we have used ourselves and rated, and paid tiers that climb by seats and marketing contacts until the Professional cliff at $800 a month billed annually ($890 on monthly billing). Its real rivals are other CRM platforms, so those are what it is compared against here. Pick a rival to see the verdict for that pairing, and follow the deep comparisons for the three pairings that deserve the full treatment.

HubSpot vs Salesforce

What it isThe original CRM platform. Immense depth, customisation and ecosystem, priced and administered like enterprise software.
Pricing shapePer user, per month, and the bill grows with editions and add ons. HubSpot grows by seats and marketing contacts instead.
The honest gapSalesforce can be shaped into almost anything, if you have the admin time or budget. HubSpot works out of the box and charges you later, at the tier cliff.

Choose HubSpot if you want one friendly system for marketing, sales and service that a small team can run without an administrator.

Choose Salesforce if your sales process is complex enough to need deep custom objects, heavy reporting, or an enterprise ecosystem, and you can resource running it.

HubSpot vs Zoho

What it isA forty plus app suite with a capable CRM at its centre, sold at prices HubSpot cannot get near.
Pricing shapePer user, and Zoho One covers the whole suite for roughly the cost of a single HubSpot seat. HubSpot adds $50 a month per extra 1,000 marketing contacts on Starter.
The honest gapZoho wins on value and breadth. HubSpot wins on polish, ease, and how much of it a beginner can use without help.

Choose HubSpot if ease of use and a joined up marketing platform matter more than the monthly saving, and your list is growing into paid tiers anyway.

Choose Zoho if budget is the binding constraint and you are willing to trade polish for a suite that covers CRM, accounts, projects and more.

HubSpot vs Pipedrive

What it isA sales pipeline tool, deliberately narrow. It manages deals beautifully and does almost nothing else.
Pricing shapePer user, cheaper than HubSpot at every comparable level, because it is selling one job rather than a platform.
The honest gapIf your problem is purely a pipeline, Pipedrive is the calmer buy. The moment marketing email, landing pages and service tickets join the picture, you are bolting tools together.

Choose HubSpot if the CRM has to serve marketing and service as well as sales, in one place, with one contact record.

Choose Pipedrive if you need a pipeline your salespeople will actually keep updated, and nothing else from a CRM.

HubSpot vs Freshsales

What it isFreshworks’ CRM: HubSpot’s shape at a lower price, with built in phone, chat and AI assistance.
Pricing shapePer user, undercutting HubSpot tier for tier, with a usable free plan.
The honest gapFreshsales is the value pick of the CRM shaped tools. HubSpot’s marketing depth and app ecosystem stay ahead.

Choose HubSpot if marketing is half the reason you are buying a CRM, not an afterthought.

Choose Freshsales if you want the all in one CRM shape on a tighter budget and can live with a smaller ecosystem.

HubSpot vs Copper

What it isA CRM that lives inside Google Workspace, so the interface is your Gmail inbox.
Pricing shapePer user, mid market. No separate marketing contact charges, because marketing is not its job.
The honest gapFor a Google first team, Copper removes the CRM adoption problem almost entirely. It will not run your marketing.

Choose HubSpot if you need marketing, sales and service in one platform, not just contact management.

Choose Copper if your team lives in Gmail and every previous CRM died of neglect.

HubSpot vs Keap

What it isCRM, automation and invoicing aimed squarely at very small service businesses.
Pricing shapePriced by contacts and users, generally below HubSpot once HubSpot’s contact blocks stack up.
The honest gapKeap is simpler to own and includes payments. HubSpot outgrows it on marketing and reporting depth.

Choose HubSpot if you are building a marketing led business that will need serious tooling within two years.

Choose Keap if you run a small service firm and want follow up, appointments and invoices handled without a platform project.

HubSpot vs monday CRM

What it isThe CRM layer of the monday.com work platform: boards and automations wearing a CRM hat.
Pricing shapePer seat in monday’s usual bundles, with the three seat minimum monday applies across its paid plans.
The honest gapIf the team already runs its work in monday, the CRM is a short step. As a standalone CRM purchase, HubSpot is the more complete tool.

Choose HubSpot if the CRM is the purchase, and marketing depth matters.

Choose monday CRM if your team already lives in monday and a CRM inside it beats a better CRM nobody opens.

Leaving HubSpot: the exit costs

The contract. Annual plans run for the term. Downgrades, seat cuts and tier reductions take effect at renewal, not mid term, so an overbought tier is a cost you carry to the end of the year.

The contacts ratchet. Marketing contacts are billed in tiers. Grow past your tier and the bill steps up; it does not step back down mid term if the list shrinks again. Mark contacts you never email as non marketing contacts, and clean the list before it cleans out your budget.

What leaves with you. Contacts, companies, deals and tickets export to CSV, and your files can be downloaded. The records are yours.

What stays behind. Workflows, sequences, forms, landing pages, custom reports and email engagement history do not move to the next tool. They are rebuilt from scratch, or they are gone.

The habit that protects you. Export the database quarterly and keep a copy outside HubSpot. Then leaving is a migration project, not a hostage negotiation.
